The recipe book.

Six scent-free recipes, easiest first. Weights are in grams, lye amounts are pre-calculated, and every recipe uses only what's in the starter order plus groceries. The full method lives with recipe nº 1; the others note where they differ.

The rules for every single batch
  1. Run the numbers through soapcalc.net before you pour, every time. Enter the oils and superfat, confirm the lye weight matches within a gram. This habit is what keeps soap safe.
  2. Full gear from lye-mixing to cleanup: sealed goggles, nitrile gloves, sleeves. Lye into water, never water into lye. No aluminum tools, ever.
  3. Combine oils and lye solution at 38-43°C, within about ten degrees of each other.
  4. Log every batch: number, date, weights, temperatures, anything odd.
  5. New ingredient = new formula. Check it against the Health Canada Hotlist first, and each formula needs its own notification after first sale.
  6. Cure on a rack with airflow. Zap test at week two. Nothing sells before six weeks.

Trace, molding, cutting, and troubleshooting are covered step by step in the soap-making guide.

Recipe nº 2

The Coconut

one oil, taken seriously

BeginnerMoves fast~7 barsCure 6 weeks

Bright white with huge bubbly lather; it even works in hard water. The high superfat is what keeps a pure coconut bar from being drying, so do not "correct" the 20% down to 5%.

Coconut oil (RBD 76)500 g
Lye (NaOH), 20% superfat73.2 g
Distilled water146-150 g

Coconut traces quickly and heats up hard. Blend in short bursts, pour at thin trace, insulate lightly or not at all, and cut on day 1-2 before it turns rock hard.

LabelSodium Cocoate, Glycerin, Water/Eau, Cocos Nucifera (Coconut) Oil

Recipe nº 4

The Porridge

the sensitive-skin flagship

Beginner + one step~7 barsCure 6 weeks

Recipe nº 1 with finely ground oats, the classic gentle-skin additive, and still completely scent-free. Say "with colloidal oatmeal, gentle on skin." Never say it treats eczema; that's a drug claim and it's the fastest way into trouble.

Olive oil350 g
Coconut oil (RBD 76)150 g
Lye (NaOH), 5% superfat70.6 g
Distilled water140-155 g
Rolled oats, ground to flour15 g

Blitz the oats to powder first; coarse oats scratch and go slimy. Stir in by hand at light trace, then pour. Expect a speckled, cream-coloured bar.

LabelSodium Olivate, Sodium Cocoate, Glycerin, Water/Eau, Avena Sativa (Oat) Kernel Flour

Recipe nº 5

The Shoreline

the coconut salt bar, a premium SKU

Intermediate: timing~8 barsCut at 2-4 hCure 6+ weeks

Dense and smooth like a beach stone, with a lotion-y lather. Salt kills suds, which is why this is coconut-heavy; coconut is the only oil that pushes lather through it. Sells at $10-12.

Coconut oil (RBD 76)400 g
Olive oil75 g
Castor oil25 g
Lye (NaOH), 20% superfat69.2 g
Distilled water138-145 g
Fine sea salt250 g

Fine sea salt only: never coarse (scratchy), never Dead Sea (weeps). Stir in at light trace, pour fast, and cut while still warm at 2-4 hours; overnight it shatters instead of slicing. Individual cavity molds skip the deadline entirely. It keeps improving for months.

LabelSodium Cocoate, Sodium Olivate, Sodium Castorate, Sea Salt (Maris Sal), Glycerin, Water/Eau

Recipe nº 6

The Heirloom

pure Castile, the patience project

Beginner to makeCure 6-12 months~7 bars

100% olive oil, the two-thousand-year-old recipe, absurdly gentle. The catch: it's soft and slimy young, and only becomes wonderful with age. Pour a loaf now, sell it next spring as a limited aged bar.

Olive oil500 g
Lye (NaOH), 5% superfat63.7 g
Distilled water127-135 g

Trace takes ten minutes or more of patient bursts; it's ready when it's ready. Date the loaf and forget about it.

LabelSodium Olivate, Glycerin, Water/Eau

The order of attack

  1. Nº 1 this week: learn the process. It becomes The Original.
  2. Nº 2 the following week: learn how a fast batter feels. It becomes The Coconut.
  3. Nº 4 when nº 1 felt comfortable: same recipe plus oats, and the strongest fit for the brand.
  4. Nº 3 as the first big production loaf in October.
  5. Nº 5 and nº 6 when you're having fun: one becomes the premium bar, the other becomes next year's story.

Every recipe here shares one label promise: no fragrance, no dye, nothing that needs an asterisk.
